### The Big Picture

Data Skaters is a hands-on learning experience that introduces students in Grades 5 - 12 to data literacy and data soverignty through hockey. Students explore the Indigenous principles of OCAP® (Ownership, Control, Access, and Possession), and learn how data can be collected, analyzed, and interpreted. The program focuses on skills in numeracy, coding, computational thinking, critical thinking, team work, collaboration, and AI literacy.

Data Skaters is flexible and adaptable to any schedule, space, or resource setup. Teachers can combine, simplify, or reorder activites as needed while maintaining core outcomes: data literacy, critical thinking, collaboration, and responsible data use.

## Set-Up

This works best in a large space such as a gymnasium, or outdoors if weather permits.

Students should be in groups of six or fewer. Each group will need:

* an internet-connected device with a camera, such as a laptop or tablet
* a hockey net (mini, full-size with part blocked off, or a target on the wall)
* a mini stick or full-size stick
* a foam ball or soft puck
* masking tape or painter's tape and a marker
* distance markings in front of the net at approximately 2 feet, 5 feet, 7 feet, and 10 feet
* measuring tape
  * (optional) vertical tape on the wall with markings at 10 cm increments for measuring height
  * (optional) horizontal tape on the wall with markings at 10 cm increments for measuring wingspan
* (optional) pulse oximeter or similar device for measuring heart rate
* (optional) paper and pencils for recording data before entering it into the digital forms

As well, you will need:

* reliable internet access
  * ensure that the [Data Skaters Website](https://datadunkers.ca/skaters) is not blocked, also test that [card creator](https://datadunkers.ca/skaters/card-creator/index.html?testing=true) renders correctly
* projector or screen for displaying slides and data visualizations
* supplies for creating player cards
  * colour printer
  * scissors or paper cutters
  * card protectors (hard plastic or soft)
  * tables for assembling and displaying the cards
* (optional) for esports data collection:
  * laptops or computer lab with [Rocket League](https://www.rocketleague.com) installed
  * gaming controllers (two per station for 1v1 games)
  * [Epic Games](https://store.epicgames.com) accounts logged in on the computers
* signed media consent forms if you are sharing photos of the event

**Important**: Decide on an *event key* (e.g. MFNSS) and input it into each of the laptops to ensure that all of the collected data is going to the same database.

## Introduction

Starting with the end in mind, show students a [player card](https://datadunkers.ca/skaters/card-creator/index.html?demo=true) as an example of how their data will be used.

* They will be taking their own photo and deciding on a four-character nickname.
* The card will include the data the collect about their traits and shot accuracy.
* There will also be visualizations on the back about how their archetype was determined, and correlations in some of the data that will be collected today.
  * The player archetype is a data-informed profile that describes a pattern in a student's results. It is based only on the activities and attributes included in the Data Skaters data set, so it should be used for reflection and discussion rather than as a complete description of the student. The archetypes are Sniper, Speed Winger, Playmaker, Defenseman, Power Forward, and Two-way Forward.

The steps for the day are:

1. Introduce the supporters, the purpose of the day, and how data science, data sovereignty, and artificial intelligence are related.
2. Take a player photo at their data collection station (hockey net) using the [photo booth](https://datadunkers.ca/skaters/photobooth).
3. Record their [traits](https://datadunkers.ca/skaters/traits) (measurements) such as height, wingspan, resting heart rate, and reaction time.
4. Take [shots](https://datadunkers.ca/skaters/shots) on a hockey net from four different distances and record their success rate.
5. (Optional) Play a round of Rocket League and record their individual [esports results](https://datadunkers.ca/skaters/rocketleague) such as goals and assists.
6. Play at least one game on "fair teams" as determined by AI from their collected data.
7. Visualize and analyze the group data and their own data.
8. Discuss the archetypes and get a unique player card with their data.

### OCAP® and Data Sovereignty

Developed in 1998 by the First Nations Information Governance Centre (FNIGC), OCAP® is a set of foundational principles that establishes First Nations data sovereignty—the right of First Nations communities to own, control, access, and possess data collected about their people, culture, and lands.

In Data Skaters, OCAP® principles are directly integrated:
* Ownership: While gathering data to create player cards, students evaluate who holds the rights to an athlete's performance data, health metrics, and digital representation.
* Control: Students analyze who has the authority to decide how statistics are interpreted, categorized, and presented, such as when determining player archetypes.
* Access: Students discuss who should be granted permission to view detailed versus high-level player statistics, balancing transparency with privacy.
* Possession: Students consider where data is stored, who is responsible for keeping it secure, and what happens to it after the player cards have been generated.

The term [data sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_sovereignty) relates to a nation's regulations and control over data generated within its borders. It can be related to transnational data flow or the relationship of Indigenous groups to post-colonial states.

All of the data collected is either stored locally or on a Canadian server managed by the Data Skaters team. Participant data will not be retained.

## Data Collection

Each student should decide on a player ID (nickname) with two letters and a two-digit number (e.g. PS43). Students will be using the same nickname all day, so consider writing it on a piece of masking tape for them to use like a nametag.

Divide students into groups, and ensure they know their group number (e.g. tape label on the hockey net).
